## Who we serve

Nine kinds of work. Each can be its own page.

### B2B SaaS and platforms

A first version a customer can actually use: login, first screen, and a bill. Each customer has their own space. Plans are written in plain words. Card payments stay with a payment company the client already trusts.

A morning signup lands ready to use. A teammate can invite a client who only sees their own work. The bill matches what the product did.

For a first product: one job, a login, and a way to pay, showable in about two weeks. For something already live: clearer access as more people join.

### AI helpers

A helper connected to the business’s own pages, prices, and calendar. It answers, books, and files. If the site does not say it, the helper should not say it. When it is unsure, a person sees the question and what was already said. Invoices and forms become fields. A weekly view shows where the helper stepped aside.

### Web3 screens

The part people see before they confirm: connect, review, wait, done. Each state has a sentence. What will move, to whom, and what it costs is visible before approval. Rejected, dropped, or wrong network is explained, with a next step. A specialist still checks the contract itself. This studio builds the interface, not a promise about the contract.

### E-commerce and D2C

A shop that loads properly, explains shipping on the product page, and stops selling a size that is gone. Someone can start on a phone and pay on a laptop without refilling the bag. Fit, delivery, and returns sit with the product. Checkout asks for less, and the confirmation says what happens next.

### Growth funnels

Do not send every ad to the homepage. Each offer gets its own page, a short form, and a way to see what worked. The form asks what you need to reply, not a biography. The thank-you says when a person will reply.

### Real estate and hospitality

A listing that feels like arriving, and a lead that does not wait until Monday. Photos, plan, light, location, and price in an order a thumb can follow. WhatsApp or a short form already names the property. Viewings and stays book against real availability on the same page.

### FinTech-style products

Explain the product before asking for details. An advisor and a client should not see the same things. Payments stay with a known processor. Actions leave a trail a human can read. Do not invent a certification or a compliance badge.

### Healthcare

Booking and follow-up that are obvious on a phone. Patients see a time. Staff see the day. Same calendar. Forms are short, can save progress, and can be read without a clinician beside them. Front desk, clinician, and patient do not share one login with everything exposed. Follow the privacy rules the client gives you. Do not put a compliance badge on the homepage.

### Cloud and DevOps

Hosting a small team can run: backups, a simple publish path, and an alert when the site is actually down. Push, build, release. If the release is wrong, the previous one is still there. What the machine runs, where the data lives, and who can log in is written down. The new setup runs beside the old one until the cutover is boring.
